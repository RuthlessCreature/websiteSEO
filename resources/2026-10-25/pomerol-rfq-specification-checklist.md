# RFQ specification checklist for China-sourced products

**Status: editorial draft; not published.** Use it to make a request for quotation more comparable. It does not verify a factory, establish product compliance or substitute for a buyer's engineering, legal, quality or customs review.

## 1. Identify the product and intended use

- Product name, internal part number and controlled drawing/specification revision
- Intended application and operating environment
- Buyer, final user and delivery destination/country
- Expected annual demand and first-order quantity, with forecast clearly marked as an estimate
- Required quotation currency, Incoterms® rule/version and named place if the buyer has already selected them
- Expected RFQ decision date and requested shipment window

## 2. State critical requirements

For each feature, identify whether it is critical, preferred, or informational. Provide the measurable value, tolerance, unit, test or inspection method, reference document and approving person. Avoid words such as “high quality,” “standard,” “equivalent” or “fast” without an agreed definition.

Attach drawings and photos only when the sender has permission to share them. Identify proprietary information and the intended recipients.

## 3. Keep revisions and variants under control

- List every SKU, size, color, plug/connector, voltage, material or packaging variant.
- Assign a drawing/BOM revision to each variant.
- Identify the product characteristic that distinguishes variants on the physical item, carton, label and order records.
- State which changes require written buyer approval before production.
- Name the party that can release a change and the version of that approval to retain.

## 4. Plan sample approval and production verification

| Stage | Questions to settle before ordering |
|---|---|
| Prototype/sample | Quantity, cost responsibility, sample marking, evaluation method, owner and acceptance window |
| Pre-production sample | Is written approval required before production? Which exact revision will the sample represent? |
| In-process checks | Which characteristics, at which steps, by whom, with what records? |
| Pre-shipment inspection | Lot definition, sampling method, defect categories, inspection criteria, date, access and report format |
| Nonconformance | Hold/rework/replacement decision owner, evidence, timing and disposition authority |
| Shipment release | Who confirms final quantity, labels, cartons, documents and dispatch authorization? |

The buyer and qualified quality representative must select appropriate sample sizes, acceptance limits and test methods for the product and risk. Do not assume an AQL or certification requirement without buyer confirmation.

## 5. Request a comparable supplier quote

Ask each respondent to state assumptions separately from confirmed facts:

- Legal supplier identity and the manufacturing/assembly location relevant to this item
- Whether a quoted process is performed by the supplier or a named subcontractor
- Tooling, minimum order quantity, sample and setup charges, each shown separately
- Unit price breaks by quantity; quote validity date and currency
- Production lead-time assumptions, capacity constraints and planned calendar
- Packing method, carton details, shipping unit and named delivery point assumptions
- Quality records, tests or market documents the supplier can actually provide
- Exclusions, open questions, substitute materials/processes and proposed deviations

Buyers should verify supplier identity, scope, technical evidence and applicable market rules through independent, appropriately qualified checks. A quotation or supplier answer alone is not verification.

## Reader next step

Send suppliers one controlled specification revision, ask each to identify deviations line-by-line, and keep unknown costs, test methods and lead-time assumptions visible for the buying decision.

## English page draft

# China company setup for foreigners: plan the route before filing

Setting up a business in China is a sequence of decisions, not a single application form. The right starting point depends on what the business will actually do, where it will operate, who will invest, what approvals or licences may apply, and whether a founder also intends to work or live in China.

This guide helps foreign founders and overseas businesses organize those questions before committing to an address, a filing route or a service provider. It is general information, not a legal conclusion for a particular investor or city. Confirm the current requirements with the responsible local registration authority and qualified advisers before acting.

## Start with the business activity and location

Write down the actual products or services, intended customers, revenue model, operational activities and first operating location. Broad labels such as “consulting,” “technology” or “trading” may not describe the real activity precisely enough to assess the applicable registration scope or industry permissions.

Next, identify the city and district where the company would be registered and managed. Registration procedures and document handling are administered locally. For example, Beijing’s official investment service explains its registration channel and lists application materials such as the registration form, articles of association, investor qualification documents, appointment records and premises documents. That is a Beijing example; it should not be treated as a national checklist for every city.

## Check market access before choosing an entity

Review whether the planned activity appears in a current foreign-investment access restriction or prohibition, and whether sector-specific approvals, permits or operating qualifications apply. The national Foreign Investment Access Negative List is one source for special restrictions; it does not replace a review of other laws, licensing rules or local procedures.

The 2024 national Negative List took effect on 1 November 2024. A business should check the current list and the full rules for its actual activity at the time of planning. Do not assume that an activity is unrestricted simply because it is absent from one list.

## Compare the possible operating forms

China’s current legal framework generally organizes foreign-invested enterprises as companies or partnerships under the applicable company and partnership laws. A limited liability company is one common form, but it is not automatically the right structure for every investor, business or liability profile. The investment authority’s 2025 guide covers enterprise incorporation and modification, taxation, foreign exchange, customs and foreign-investment information reporting.

A representative office is different: official local guidance describes it as a non-profit office for liaison, market research, exhibitions and publicity activities related to a foreign enterprise. It is not a substitute for an operating company where the intended activity is profit-making in China. Confirm the exact permitted scope with the registration authority and a qualified adviser.

“WFOE” remains widely used as a search term, but it can obscure the current legal and registration framework. Use the investor’s actual ownership and operating plan to compare available structures instead of selecting a form from an old label alone.

## Build a document and decision checklist

Before filing, ask the local registration authority or adviser to confirm the current form and supporting documents for the selected city, entity and activity. A planning file commonly needs to resolve:

- Investor identity and qualification documents, including translation, notarization or authentication requirements where applicable.
- Proposed Chinese and English company names, business scope and principal place of business.
- Ownership, subscribed capital, contribution schedule and governance arrangements.
- Appointment and role information for the legal representative, directors, supervisors or other required positions.
- Evidence or documentation relating to the premises and address.
- Any sector approval or licence required before or after registration.
- The process for foreign-investment information reporting and later tax, foreign-exchange, customs or employer registrations that apply to the actual operation.

This is a question list, not a complete or universally applicable document list. For instance, official Beijing FAQs identify particular submission materials for Beijing company or partnership registrations; another city may use different digital channels, forms or supporting-document rules.

## Keep company registration and the founder’s personal route separate

Registering an entity does not itself settle a foreign founder’s right to work or reside in China. If the founder plans to perform work in China, assess the real role, employer, location and personal circumstances separately under the applicable work and residence procedures. Do not assume that owning shares, holding a business licence or appointing oneself to a role automatically provides work authorization.

Before making commitments, confirm the personal route with the competent authority or a qualified provider for the specific city and situation. Avoid sending passport scans, police certificates or other sensitive documents through an unverified channel during an initial inquiry.

## Questions to resolve before paying for an address or filing

1. What business activity will the entity actually conduct, and who will be its customers?
2. Which city and district will host the real operation, and what local premises evidence is accepted?
3. Does the activity require a sector licence, prior approval or other qualification?
4. Which entity form fits the proposed ownership, governance, funding and liability arrangements?
5. What are the current capital, contribution timing and governance requirements for that form?
6. Which documents require translation, notarization or authentication, and who will confirm this for the filing location?
7. Will the founder work in China, and what separate work-permit and residence questions must be checked?
8. Which post-registration tax, foreign-exchange, customs, banking and employer steps apply to the real operation?
